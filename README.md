# Food Delivery Data Visualization Portfolio 

## Project Overview

This project presents a data visualization portfolio for a Food Delivery Business Performance Dataset using Python, Matplotlib, and Seaborn.

The objective is to explore and communicate meaningful patterns in food delivery business performance through appropriate visualizations. The analysis focuses on orders, revenue, marketing spend, cities, cuisines, delivery time, customer ratings, weather conditions, and order channels.

## Dataset

The dataset contains **360 records and 16 columns**.

### Dataset Features

* `Order_Batch_ID` – Unique order batch identifier
* `Date` – Date of the order batch
* `Month` – Month of the order
* `Day` – Day of the week
* `City` – City where the orders were placed
* `Cuisine` – Cuisine category
* `Order_Channel` – Channel through which the order was received
* `Weather` – Weather condition
* `Orders` – Number of orders
* `Average_Order_Value` – Average value of an order
* `Revenue` – Revenue generated
* `Marketing_Spend` – Marketing expenditure
* `Discounts` – Discounts provided
* `Avg_Delivery_Minutes` – Average delivery time
* `Customer_Rating` – Customer satisfaction rating
* `Repeat_Customer_Percent` – Percentage of repeat customers

## Visualizations Created

### 1. Orders Over Time

A line plot was used to visualize changes in order volume over time.

**Purpose:** Identify periods of high and low order demand.

### 2. Revenue Over Time

A line plot was used to examine changes in revenue across dates.

**Purpose:** Understand revenue trends and compare them with changes in order volume.

### 3. Monthly Orders

A bar chart was used to compare total orders across months.

**Purpose:** Identify months with relatively higher or lower demand.

### 4. Revenue by City

A bar chart was used to compare total revenue across cities.

**Purpose:** Identify the strongest-performing locations.

### 5. Orders by Cuisine

A bar chart was used to compare order volumes across cuisine categories.

**Purpose:** Understand customer preferences for different cuisines.

### 6. Marketing Spend vs Revenue

A scatter plot was created to investigate the relationship between marketing expenditure and revenue.

**Purpose:** Determine whether higher marketing spend is associated with higher revenue.

### 7. Delivery Time vs Customer Rating

A scatter plot was used to examine the relationship between average delivery time and customer ratings.

**Purpose:** Investigate whether delivery speed is associated with customer satisfaction.

### 8. Orders Distribution

A histogram was used to visualize the distribution of order volumes.

**Purpose:** Understand the typical range of orders and identify unusual observations.

### 9. Revenue Distribution

A histogram was used to examine the distribution of revenue.

**Purpose:** Identify the typical revenue range and potential extreme values.

### 10. Revenue by City

A box plot was used to compare revenue distributions across cities.

**Purpose:** Compare median revenue, variability, and potential outliers between locations.

### 11. Customer Rating by Cuisine

A violin plot was used to examine the distribution of customer ratings for different cuisines.

**Purpose:** Understand differences in customer satisfaction and rating variability.

### 12. Order Channel Analysis

A count plot was used to compare the frequency of different order channels.

**Purpose:** Identify the most frequently used ordering channels.

### 13. Revenue by Order Channel

A bar chart was used to compare revenue generated by different order channels.

**Purpose:** Determine which channels contribute most to business revenue.

### 14. Weather vs Orders

A box plot was used to compare order volumes under different weather conditions.

**Purpose:** Investigate whether weather affects food delivery demand.

### 15. Weather vs Revenue

A bar chart was used to compare average revenue under different weather conditions.

**Purpose:** Identify potential differences in business performance caused by weather conditions.

### 16. Repeat Customer Percentage by City

A box plot was used to compare repeat-customer percentages between cities.

**Purpose:** Understand differences in customer retention across locations.

### 17. Discounts vs Revenue

A scatter plot was used to investigate the relationship between discounts and revenue.

**Purpose:** Determine whether higher discount levels are associated with changes in revenue.

### 18. Correlation Heatmap

A correlation heatmap was created for numerical variables.

**Purpose:** Identify strong positive and negative relationships between business performance variables.

## Key Findings

1. Order volumes and revenue vary over time, indicating changes in food delivery demand across different periods.

2. Revenue performance differs between cities, showing that some locations contribute more strongly to overall business performance.

3. Cuisine categories have different order volumes, indicating differences in customer preferences.

4. Marketing spend can be compared with revenue to identify whether increased marketing activity is associated with stronger revenue performance.

5. Delivery time and customer ratings provide useful information about the relationship between operational efficiency and customer satisfaction.

6. Different order channels contribute differently to overall business activity and revenue.

7. Weather conditions can influence food delivery demand and revenue, making external conditions an important factor to consider.

8. The correlation heatmap helps identify important relationships among orders, revenue, average order value, marketing spend, delivery time, customer ratings, and repeat customers.

## Conclusion

The Food Delivery Data Visualization Portfolio demonstrates how different visualization techniques can be selected according to the question being investigated.

Line plots were useful for time-based trends, bar charts for category comparisons, scatter plots for relationships between variables, histograms for distributions, box plots for comparing distributions and detecting outliers, violin plots for detailed distribution analysis, count plots for categorical frequencies, and heatmaps for correlation analysis.

The analysis provides a visual understanding of the factors affecting food delivery business performance and demonstrates the use of Matplotlib and Seaborn for effective exploratory data visualization.
